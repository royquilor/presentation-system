# Change Impact Analysis
> Identifying specific changes across people, process, and technology for the Group IT transition

**Author:** Muskan Verma
**Date:** 22 November 2025
**Source:** https://datacomgroup.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/GITTSP/pages/40419393868

## 🎯 Context

This document identifies the specific changes that will be faced in the Group IT Transition to ServiceNow (GITTSP). Changes are categorised as people, process, or technology. Results are used for the Communication and Training Plan.

---

## 🔍 Problem

The transition from Cherwell to ServiceNow introduces significant changes across people, process, and technology. Understanding impact levels (High/Medium/Low) is essential for effective change management, training, and readiness planning.

---

## 📋 Observations

**1. Access and data**
Current state: Multiple disconnected tenancies, inconsistent role-based access, siloed CMDB data. Future state: Unified CMDB with governed role-based access, ensuring consistency and security. Impact: People M, Process H, Technology H.

**2. Ticketing**
Current state: Requests initiated via email, limited visibility, manual follow-ups. Future state: Requests submitted via portal, supported by virtual agent and integrated knowledge base. Impact: Shift from email-only to self-service portal and AI-driven support. People H, Process H, Technology H.

**3. Routing and categorisation**
Current state: Tickets manually assigned and categorised, prone to delays and errors. Future state: Automated assignment based on CI data, SLA tracking embedded. Impact: Automation of ticket triage and SLA enforcement. People M, Process H, Technology H.

**4. Knowledge management**
Current state: Knowledge scattered across different repositories, no unified governance. Future state: Single repository with structured search, tagging, and review workflows. Impact: Centralisation and governance of knowledge base. People M, Process H, Technology M.

**5. Reporting**
Current state: Basic, limited reporting capabilities, often retrospective. Future state: Real-time dashboards, trend analysis, actionable insights for decision-making. Impact: Enhanced visibility and analytics. People L, Process M, Technology H.

**6. Problem management**
Current state: Focus on resolving incidents reactively, minimal root cause analysis. Future state: Proactive identification of issues, prevention-focused problem management. Impact: Shift from reactive to proactive ITSM practices. People M, Process H, Technology M.

**7. Highest impact areas**
Ticketing and routing/categorisation show High impact across all three dimensions (People, Process, Technology). Access and data show High impact for Process and Technology.

**8. Change management focus**
Six change areas require attention; ticketing and routing have the highest impact and should be prioritised for communication and training.

---

## 💡 Proposal

Use the Change Impact Analysis to inform the Communication and Training Plan. Prioritise high-impact areas for targeted change management.

- Prioritise ticketing and routing changes for training and readiness
- Address access and data consolidation for resolver groups
- Plan knowledge management centralisation and governance
- Ensure reporting and problem management capabilities are understood

*The analysis provides a structured framework for change readiness across people, process, and technology dimensions.*

---

## ⚠️ Risks

- **High-impact ticketing change:** Users must adapt from email to portal and virtual agent—resistance or confusion could slow adoption.
- **Process automation:** Automated routing and SLA enforcement may require significant process adjustment for resolver teams.

---

## ✅ Next Steps

1. **Communication plan** — Use impact analysis to sequence and tailor communications for each change area.
2. **Training plan** — Develop role-based training for high-impact areas (ticketing, routing, knowledge).
3. **Readiness assessment** — Validate resolver group readiness for process and technology changes.
