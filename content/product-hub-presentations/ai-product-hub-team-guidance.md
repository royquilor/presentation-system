# AI Product Hub — Team Guidance
> Building an internal directory of AI, automation, and analytics capabilities to make the team's work visible across Datacom

**Author:** Joe Thornley
**Date:** March 2026
**Source:** https://datacomgroup.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/IA

---

## 🎯 Context

The Insights & Analytics team has built dozens of AI capabilities — from Conver to TACO to the Agent Library.
Most of this work is invisible to leadership and other teams.
The Product Hub makes it visible. Inventory everything first. Selection happens later.

---

## 🔍 Problem

Most organisations appear to have only a few visible AI initiatives.
In reality, there are often dozens under development.
Without a single directory, leadership can't see what exists. Teams duplicate effort. Opportunities are missed.

---

## 📋 Observations

**1. Objective**
Build a single internal AI capability showcase.
Demonstrate the team already operates as an AI lab with real outputs.
Show the pipeline from experiments through to products.
Help leadership understand what exists, who it's for, and its value.

**2. Scope — all work, not just production**
Production capabilities (Conver, contract analysis, dashboards).
Active pilots (agentic BA, knowledge generation, AI workflow automation).
POCs and experiments (MCP integrations, model prototypes).
Delivery accelerators (ServiceNow bots, proposal generators).
Analytics (operational dashboards, financial insights).

**3. Five capability categories**
AI Products — for operational or commercial use.
Automation — workflows, scripts, and tools.
Analytics — dashboards, models, decision-support.
Delivery Accelerators — tools that improve delivery speed or quality.
Experiments — prototypes and technical explorations.

**4. Four status labels**
Production — live and actively used.
Pilot — limited user group validating value.
POC — technical feasibility demonstrated.
One-off — built for a specific situation. Still valuable as evidence of capability.

**5. L0–L4 maturity framework**
L0 Idea → L1 POC → L2 Pilot → L3 Operational Capability → L4 Product Offering.
Each level has defined promotion gates.
POC → Pilot: use case, users, hypothesis.
Pilot → Operational: owner, infrastructure, access, usage.
Operational → Product: value prop, delivery model, audience, scalable architecture.

**6. The 6-section product page framework**
What It Is (150 words). Who It's For (200 words). Why It Matters (300 words).
How to Use It (500 words, 3+ steps). Examples (500 words, 2+ use cases). Run It (100 words).
Goal: understand and use any tool in under 15 minutes — no meeting required.

**7. 11-question intake questionnaire**
Name. Category. Status. One-sentence description. Built for whom.
Problem solved. Evidence of value. Technology used. Owner.
Links or artefacts. Next step.
Target: Jason's AI team, Matt's analytics team, Harrison, engineers, experimenters.

**8. MVP — visibility first, polish later**
Ship the directory with capability pages, status labels, and maturity levels.
Defer training videos, long-form docs, architecture diagrams, and case studies.
Priority: make the pipeline visible. Iterate on quality after launch.

---

## 💡 Proposal

Distribute the questionnaire. Collect submissions. Classify by category, status, and maturity. Publish the directory. Present to leadership.
